## Did that money build anything, or just buy it?

Mostly, it changed the name on the share register of existing companies in India.

A greenfield investment adds new factories and machines to India, while an acquisition simply hands ownership of an existing Indian asset to a buyer abroad. In 2018, net foreign acquisitions of Indian companies reached $33.6 billion, dominated by the Walmart acquisition of Flipkart, against $42.2 billion of recorded foreign direct investment. During the investment peak in 2020, foreign acquisitions took up $21.8 billion against $64.1 billion of recorded inflows.

These two lines move on different clocks in India. Because deal values and balance-of-payments flows come from different sources with different timing conventions, acquisitions compiled by UNCTAD are not a clean subset of recorded inflows. But their relative shapes show how foreign interest in India shifts. In 2024, net foreign acquisitions fell to minus $1.4 billion, and in 2025 they fell further to minus $3.3 billion, their lowest point since 1990. This means foreign firms sold more Indian assets than they bought in the very years that produced the 44 per cent rebound in recorded inflows.

What is left is the greenfield pipeline of new projects in India. When foreign firms withdraw from existing acquisitions, the remaining inflows entering India must represent new capacity, even if the transition is slow.
