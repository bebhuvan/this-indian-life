## What is RBI reviewing, and how big is LRS?

Resident individuals sent $28.98 billion abroad under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) in FY2025-26. Paying for a university, supporting a relative and buying a foreign share can all use this window. Tightening one category can affect very different people from tightening another.

Late-October 2026 press reports described a possible Rs 50 lakh annual sub-limit on selected remittances, including business travel and pilgrimage, with education and medical treatment potentially exempt. These are reported proposals. We have not found an implementing notification from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). The published rules still allow eligible resident individuals, including minors, to remit up to $250,000 per financial year for permitted transactions, subject to the scheme's conditions and specific exceptions.

The monthly record covers 221 calendar months from April 2008 to August 2026. Annual outflows reached $31.74 billion in FY2023-24 before easing to $29.56 billion in FY2024-25 and $28.98 billion in FY2025-26. Outflows in August 2026 were $2.74 billion. The first five months of FY2026-27, April to August 2026, totalled $12.56 billion, up 4.5% from the corresponding period a year earlier.

For scale, gross inward private transfers were $155.12 billion in FY2025-26, while gross outward foreign direct investment (FDI) debits were $37.33 billion. Private transfers are broader than household remittances, and outward FDI includes investors beyond resident individuals. These are different, potentially overlapping flows. They cannot be added together or treated as a clean offset to LRS.

An LRS payment also appears within India's wider external accounts. Its effect on reserves or [the rupee](/articles/why-the-rupee-falls/) depends on other transactions and valuation changes. The purpose breakdown is more useful for judging which remittance rules would affect which kinds of spending.

## What do the first six months of RBI's travel breakdown reveal?

The frozen monthly export first reports separate business, pilgrimage, medical, education and other travel lines in March 2026. Through August, they add up to $7,995.32 million.

Education travel accounts for $3,322.07 million, or 41.6%. Other travel accounts for $4,478.90 million, or 56.0%. Together they make up 97.6% of the six-month total. The export calls the residual category “Other travel”; it does not identify how much is holidays, credit-card spending or any particular instrument.

Business travel and pilgrimage together account for $167.84 million, or 2.1% of travel. That is the share of spending in those categories, not the share a Rs 50 lakh per-person ceiling would prevent. We do not know how many remitters exceed the proposed limit or by how much. A cap's savings cannot be estimated from these category totals.

Education exposes another reporting trap. In August 2026, “Studies Abroad” recorded $191.84 million and “Travel for education” $855.52 million. Their combined $1,047.36 million, about $1.05 billion, was 38.2% of LRS that month. Reading the older studies line alone leaves out the separately identified education travel component. But the new split does not establish that a fall in the studies line was caused entirely by reclassification. Nor does it reconstruct comparable education totals before March 2026.

## Why are foreign equity and deposit outflows surging in FY27?

The headline increase hides a change in what the window is being used for. Across April-August 2025 and April-August 2026, total LRS rose by about $538 million. Deposits, property and equity/debt transfers together added $1.03 billion, while the remaining purposes combined fell by about $491 million. The purpose changes reconcile to the headline increase.

Equity/debt remittances rose from $822.87 million to $1,725.08 million, an increase of 109.6%. Foreign bank deposits rose 36.1%, while property purchases rose 12.7%. Those categories explain why a modest overall increase can still attract attention from regulators watching overseas investment. The export does not identify the investors' wealth, destination securities or motives.

Travel and studies are grouped together in the change chart because the new education-travel detail creates a reporting break. A decline in the direct studies line cannot establish falling demand for overseas education. Maintenance of relatives and gifts are shown separately from investment, so their decline does not disappear inside the headline.

The longer view confirms that this is more than a single busy month. The deposits, property and equity/debt total increased from $1,141.21 million in FY2019-20 to $3,933.79 million in FY2025-26. Equity/debt accounted for $2,652.01 million in the latter year.

Overseas portfolio investment operates within the LRS ceiling and the Overseas Investment rules; it is not permission to buy every foreign instrument. Under the RBI's directions, received, realised or unused foreign exchange must generally be repatriated and surrendered within 180 days unless reinvested, with the starting event specified in the rule. This is not a requirement to sell a valid investment after 180 days. Falling deposit remittances alongside rising equity/debt remittances do not establish that the same households shifted their money between them.

The RBI's July 10, 2024 International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) circular broadened the permitted purposes of remittances through foreign currency accounts in IFSCs. These are relevant legal channels, but the categories do not reveal how much went through the Gujarat International Finance Tec-City (GIFT) IFSC.

## What do the tax rules and annual calendar mean for a remitter?

Tax collected at source (TCS) can tie up cash even when it is eventually credited against income tax. Consider an eligible resident with a valid PAN making a first Rs 20 lakh other-purpose LRS remittance in the financial year, with no earlier remittances using the threshold. At the standard 20% rate on the amount above Rs 10 lakh, the bank collects Rs 2 lakh upfront. That is a tax credit, rather than a final fee for sending the money abroad. Exemptions and separate education or medical rates can change the calculation.

The collection rules have changed repeatedly. The Finance Act 2020 introduced LRS TCS, with collection taking effect on October 1, 2020. The standard rate above the threshold was 5%, with a lower rate for qualifying education loans. From October 1, 2023, other LRS purposes faced 20% on amounts above Rs 7 lakh, while education and medical treatment retained separate treatment. From April 1, 2025, the threshold rose to Rs 10 lakh and qualifying Section 80E education-loan remittances became exempt. From April 1, 2026, the education and medical rate fell from 5% to 2% under Section 394 of the new Income-tax Act, 2025. Overseas tour packages have separate rules.

The before-and-after averages diverge. Maintenance of close relatives averaged $347.8 million a month in FY2022-23 and $294.3 million from April 2025 to August 2026. Equity/debt rose from $104.7 million to $257.5 million a month across those windows. Travel rose from $1,138.5 million to $1,372.9 million. Exchange rates, reporting, incomes and travel recovery also changed. The comparison cannot isolate the tax's effect or tell us who claimed credits, split transfers across family members or abandoned a payment.

Timing is clearer when each month is measured against its own financial year's total. Across FY2022-23 to FY2025-26, March accounted for about 17% of each year's capital transfers on average. August accounted for about 14% of the direct studies line. Averaging within-year shares gives each complete year equal weight and excludes the partial latest year. Evenly spread payments would put about 8.3% in each month. The direct studies line remains an incomplete measure of education spending, and the pattern alone cannot identify why remitters acted in those months.

The ceiling has changed too. LRS began at $25,000 in 2004, rose through $50,000 and $100,000 to $200,000, fell to $75,000 in 2013, and recovered to $125,000 in 2014. It first reached $250,000 on May 26, 2015; the implementing circular followed on June 1. Bringing existing current-account allowances into LRS also changed its reporting boundary, so the increase after 2015 cannot all be read as new spending abroad.

A fixed dollar ceiling also grows in rupees when [the rupee weakens](/articles/why-the-rupee-falls/). Using the implied valuation rate in the RBI reserve series, $250,000 equalled Rs 159.13 lakh in June 2015 and Rs 237.95 lakh in August 2026, a rise of Rs 78.82 lakh, or 49.5%. This is a valuation illustration, not the exchange rate a bank would quote a customer. It does not show that households' ability to spend abroad increased relative to their incomes.

## What can LRS rules fix, and what do the numbers leave out?

The proposed travel restriction and the growth in investment remittances address different parts of LRS. Business travel and pilgrimage are small in the newly published split. Education and other travel account for most of it. The recent acceleration sits in equity and debt.

Choosing a permitted-use rule or ceiling requires information these tables do not provide: remittance counts, the distribution per person, repeat use of family allowances, destinations and compliance outcomes. A blunt limit can restrict legitimate diversification as well as unwanted transactions. The totals cannot tell us where to draw that line.

## How to read these numbers: methodology and caveats

Monthly figures use the unchanged RBI Database on Indian Economy (DBIE) Report 19 export retrieved October 25, 2026. The ACTUALS rows contain US dollars despite the measure column's display-unit label. We divide by one million for USD millions and aggregate April-March for fiscal years. FY26 means April 2025-March 2026; FY27's latest comparison covers only April-August 2026.

The raw export contains 222 distinct date strings, but only 221 calendar months: six identical category rows dated May 30, 2009 repeat rows dated May 31. We keep one identical value per month and category and reject conflicting duplicates. Three early donation cells are blank; the family/gifts composite omits those months rather than turning the blanks into measured zeros.

The change chart subtracts the same five-month window a year earlier and combines mutually exclusive main purposes. Travel and studies are grouped across the reporting change. Seasonal shares divide each month's remittances by the same channel's full-year total, then take an unweighted average across four complete financial years. The remittance-context chart compares gross flows for the same financial year, with different coverage and possible overlap. It is not a financing identity.

An independent reconciliation checks every numeric artifact cell against source CSV coordinates. Of the annual Report 752 cells compared with monthly sums, all but the FY2008-09 total agree at the displayed precision: that annual total is $124.6 million, against $808.1 million from the monthly record. The annual purpose components agree with the monthly aggregate; the disputed headline equals March alone. We use the monthly record consistently and disclose the conflict.

Education-travel and combined-education series begin in March 2026, when both components are observable. The Rs 50 lakh line is a reference benchmark, not a historical limit. There was no LRS TCS threshold before its introduction, so its line begins in October 2020. A fresh direct RBI download reproduced the frozen monthly and annual records and the original external-sector observations. Original responses and refresh receipts are retained with the source-cell audit.
