# Article rubric

The cold reader and the editor score every article draft on these eight dimensions,
0 to 3 each. The craft behind each one is in `docs/STORYTELLING.md`; numerical and
source accuracy is gated separately by `docs/STORY_PLAYBOOK.md` and is not scored
here, because it is pass or fail.

**Publish bar:** at least 18 of 24, and no dimension below 2. A 1 anywhere means
rewrite that part before anything else.

| # | Dimension | 3: publish | 1: rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Big beat** | The first two sentences state the largest true finding with a number a reader can picture. The headline and short answer say the same thing. | Opens with method, a definition, a projection or a minor finding. The reader must reach paragraph three to learn the answer. |
| 2 | **Question ladder** | Headings are questions a lay reader would ask, in order; each answer prompts the next. The first two sentences under each heading answer it. | Headings are topic labels or indicator names. Sections could be shuffled without loss. |
| 3 | **Clarity** | Every technical term is explained at first use, in plain words, and units are translated into rupees, people or shares of the population. Numbers meant to be compared share a unit and a period. No internal vocabulary. | Undefined jargon, file or field names, unexplained acronyms, dollar figures with no rupee or everyday sense. |
| 4 | **Depth without dumbing down** | Disputes, limits and uncomfortable numbers are present and explained; the writer holds a view. The reader learns where the article sits in the live debate, and gets the history where it explains the present. Boxes carry the long explanations. | Hard parts are dropped or appear only as stacked hedges; the article ignores a well-known argument about its own question; history is a timeline that explains nothing. |
| 5 | **Comprehensiveness** | The source's major India findings are used or knowingly skipped (digest skip list). Several distinct cuts (time, place, group, comparison) support the big beat. A second source fills the report's gaps. | Large parts of the source unused without reason; one cut repeated; a single source. |
| 6 | **Flow and rhythm** | Macro to specific, with a turn in each part. Section lengths vary. Number, meaning and mechanism alternate. Charts sit where their question is asked. | Equal-sized blocks; walls of method; charts in a cluster away from their prose; no turns. |
| 7 | **Honesty in the right layer** | Body sentences carry one necessary qualifier; method and replication detail sit in chart notes, boxes and the methodology section. Estimates and projections are labelled once, clearly. | Every paragraph ends in a caveat, or a caveat the reader needs is missing. |
| 8 | **Ending and macha** | Ends on the sharpest concrete point or a real open question. The macha box compresses this article's answer in plain words. | Summary restated; moral closer; boilerplate macha text that could sit under any article. |

## The cold-reader test

Separate from the score, the cold reader must be able to do these after one read of
the rendered page. Any failure is a finding.

1. State the article's answer in two sentences, with its main number.
2. Say what the main unit means in rupees or in everyday terms.
3. Name the one thing the article warns not to conclude.
4. Point to the paragraph where they first got lost, if they did.

## Reporting

Reviewers report at most ten findings, most important first, each with: dimension,
the exact sentence or section, what is wrong for the reader, and a suggested
direction (not a rewrite). The writer decides the wording.
