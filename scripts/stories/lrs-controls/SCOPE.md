# LRS controls: audited scope

Question: what does the resident-remittance record reveal about the reported October 2026 proposal, the people and purposes behind LRS, and the rules a remitter faces?

The story uses the frozen monthly LRS record for April 2008-August 2026, annual BoP through FY2025-26 for one compact scale comparison, and reserve valuation rates solely to illustrate the rupee value of the dollar ceiling. The source contains 221 calendar months. Travel subcategories begin in March 2026. The proposal is reported by the press; we have not found an implementing RBI notification.

Nine distinct visual questions are retained:

1. Full monthly LRS level and purpose groups.
2. Same-year gross LRS, outward FDI debit and inward private-transfer credit, with differences in populations and possible overlap explicit. This is a scale comparison, not a financing equation.
3. Six monthly travel compositions, preserving each business/pilgrimage/medical/education/other source value.
4. Education-related category reconciliation, only where both components are observable.
5. Complete signed contribution bridge from April-August 2025 to April-August 2026. Six exhaustive, non-overlapping groups sum to the headline change; travel and studies are grouped across the reporting break.
6. Longer history of deposits/property/equity-debt remittances.
7. Average monthly allocation within each channel's annual total. Calculate the twelve shares separately for each complete FY2022-23 to FY2025-26, then average the four fiscal years equally. Exclude partial FY27.
8. Descriptive averages across tax-policy windows, with no causal interpretation, plus an explicit first-remittance tax-credit illustration in prose.
9. Rupee valuation of the dollar ceiling and clearly labelled contextual policy thresholds.

The article does not infer cap savings, student counts, tax-credit use, individual motives or household asset substitution from aggregate categories. It does not identify all Other travel as holidays/cards or all private transfers as household remittances. Current TCS history includes the 2026 education/medical rate change and the new Act's section 394. The worked example assumes a valid PAN, an eligible first Rs20 lakh other-purpose LRS remittance in the financial year and no earlier use of the threshold; exemptions and special cases may change the calculation.
